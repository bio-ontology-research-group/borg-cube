This topic covers the structure of a paper and the process around it: the
single message, the outline, the shape of each section, authorship, venue and
the collaborative process. Sentence-level prose rules live in the separate
`write-edit-scientific-paper` skill and are not repeated here.

## What the evidence says

### One message, decided early

- Mensh and Kording's first rule: focus the paper on one central contribution and put it in the title; readers should be able to restate it to a colleague a year later, and papers that push several contributions are less convincing about each [mensh2017].
- The title is read far more often than anything else, so its quality decides whether the abstract is read; returning to the title while writing keeps the text focused on the one idea [mensh2017].
- Zhang's rule 2, less is more: fewer but more significant papers serve the community and the career better; the significance, completeness and coherence of the results as a whole decide which story to tell and what to include [zhang2014].
- Zhang's rule 1: writing is a driving force of the research, not a step after it; an outline of the paper belongs in the project design and the draft is used to reassess the logic of the experiments [zhang2014].
- Bourne's rule 7: start writing the paper the day the questions are chosen; the temptation of novice authors is to include everything they know, but a paper should impart the most information in the fewest words [bourne2005].
- Whitesides describes the outline as the working document of the research: a draft outline of the paper, agreed between student and advisor before the experiments are finished, tells which experiments are still needed and which results matter (from knowledge of the text, not verified) [whitesides2004].
- Schimel frames a paper as a story with a resolution the reader should remember, and warns that a paper without a clear single point is not cited even when it is read (from knowledge of the book) [schimel-writing-science].

### Context, content, conclusion at every scale

- The context-content-conclusion scheme applies at three scales: introduction, results and discussion for the paper; first sentence, body and last sentence for the paragraph [mensh2017].
- Papers should follow the logic of the argument, not the chronology of the work; readers care about the claim and the logic supporting it, not the path by which the authors reached it [mensh2017, zobel-writing-for-computer-science].
- Only the central idea is touched on more than once; every other subject is covered in one place, related material is kept together, and parallel ideas are given parallel form [mensh2017].
- Zhang's rule 4: settle the logical flow before any other writing tactic; a useful device is to draft the set of figures and tables first and order them so that they alone tell the story [zhang2014].
- Gopen and Swan argue that readers expect information in particular places: the subject and verb close together, old information first in the sentence, the new stressed information at the end, and one main point per unit of discourse; prose that meets these expectations is read as clear regardless of its topic (from knowledge of the text) [gopen-swan1990].
- Plaxco's central advice is that the writer's job is to make the reader's job easy: the paper should state early what was found and why it matters, and every paragraph should carry a single point the reader can restate (from knowledge of the text) [plaxco2010].
- Heard treats the paper as a document that must work for a reader who skims as well as one who reads it whole, and argues for deliberate structure over inspiration (from knowledge of the book) [heard-scientists-guide-to-writing].

### The abstract

- The abstract is the only part most readers read, so it must carry the whole message; its structure is conserved: context that narrows to the gap, "Here we" content that names the approach and summarizes the results, and a conclusion that answers the question and states the broader significance [mensh2017].
- The most common abstract mistake is to give results before the reader is ready for them; a good abstract takes many iterations until the results fill the gap like a key fits a lock [mensh2017].
- Zobel: the abstract states problem, approach and result, and carries no citations (from notes) [zobel-writing-for-computer-science].

### The introduction

- The introduction develops the gap in progressively narrower paragraphs (field gap, subfield gap, the specific gap the paper fills) and ends with a paragraph that summarizes what the paper does to fill it; it is not a literature review [mensh2017].
- The last paragraph of the introduction differs from the abstract: it omits context, is more specific about the results, and previews the conclusion only briefly if at all [mensh2017].
- Zobel: the introduction gives motivation, contribution and how the contribution is demonstrated (from notes) [zobel-writing-for-computer-science].

### Results and discussion

- Results are a sequence of declarative statements, each supported by a figure, that connect logically to the central claim; the statements become subsection headings or figure titles [mensh2017].
- Each results paragraph opens with the question it answers, presents the data and logic, and closes with the answer; this lets readers locate and check a suspicious conclusion [mensh2017].
- The first results paragraph summarizes the overall approach and key methods because most readers skip the methods section [mensh2017].
- The discussion shows how the gap was filled, gives caveats, and describes how the contribution opens future work; its first paragraph summarizes the findings, later paragraphs each take one weakness or strength and evaluate it against the literature [mensh2017].
- Zhang: interpret figures and tables rather than restate them, make the paper self-contained for the chosen audience, define uncommon terms rather than only citing them, and do not make readers do the arithmetic [zhang2014].
- Zobel: conclusions say what was learned and what remains open, not a second summary (from notes) [zobel-writing-for-computer-science].

### Where time goes

- Title, abstract and figures are seen by far more people than the rest of the paper and the methods are read least; allocate time accordingly [mensh2017].
- Outline before writing: one informal sentence per planned paragraph, starting from the descriptions of each result, so that a paragraph's role in the story is scrutinized before it is wordsmithed [mensh2017].
- Writing is an optimization of story, outline and sentences together; discarding a paragraph and rewriting is often faster than incremental editing [mensh2017].
- Test readers show where the story moves too fast or too slow; vague or unenthusiastic reviews mean the reviewers did not get the big picture, and very specific reviews point to a paragraph whose logic was insufficient [mensh2017].
- Zhang's rule 8: be your own referee; set the draft aside, then read it literally as a stranger would, and discard what fails that test [zhang2014].
- Zhang's rule 9: collect critiques from colleagues, a lab meeting or the thesis committee before submission to preempt referee concerns [zhang2014].
- Bourne: be objective about the work and have colleagues without a vested interest read the first draft rather than relying on the mentor [bourne2005].

### Audience and venue

- Zhang's rule 3: a computational biology result can be pitched to biologists, computational scientists or both; the choice of audience and journal decides the organization of the paper and its level of detail [zhang2014].
- Bourne's rule 9: decide early where to publish because it defines the form, level of detail and assumed novelty; use presubmission enquiries where available [bourne2005].
- Bourne: follow the guide to authors, since editors and reviewers do; one paper in a quality journal is worth more than several in lesser ones [bourne2005].
- Romano and Moore's rule 2: computational venues have dedicated software or database article types (Bioinformatics application notes, NAR database and web server issues, PLOS Computational Biology software articles); check nonstandard requirements such as real data or deposition rules, and match the venue's mission [romano2020].
- Romano and Moore's rule 3: publish for users, not developers; a domain journal reaches the people who will use the tool, at the cost of cutting jargon and moving technical detail to supplements or documentation [romano2020].
- Romano and Moore: a software paper is not documentation; usage examples longer than a few sentences go to a supplement or to version-controlled documentation linked from the paper, and code in the paper is a commitment to keep it working [romano2020].
- Romano and Moore: archive a versioned release with a DOI (Zenodo, FigShare) and cite it, because the median URL in a paper lives about nine years [romano2020].

### Authorship and contributions

- The ICMJE authorship criteria require all four: substantial contribution to conception, design, acquisition, analysis or interpretation; drafting or critically revising the work; approval of the final version; and agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work. Those who meet fewer criteria are acknowledged (from knowledge of the recommendations) [icmje].
- ICMJE places responsibility for deciding authorship with the authors as a group, not the journal, and asks that the corresponding author take primary responsibility for communication with the journal (from knowledge of the recommendations) [icmje].
- CRediT is a community-owned taxonomy of 14 contributor roles (conceptualization, data curation, formal analysis, funding acquisition, investigation, methodology, project administration, resources, software, supervision, validation, visualization, writing original draft, writing review and editing), approved as an ANSI/NISO standard in 2022 and licensed CC-BY 4.0 [credit].
- Frassl and colleagues' rule 4: jointly decide authorship guidelines early in the project, using discrete tasks such as ideas, funding, data, analysis, figures and text (CRediT is named); whether data provision alone earns authorship is debated, and providers who do not qualify are acknowledged [frassl2018].
- Frassl: make a statement about author order and generate attribution statements; joint first authorship is legitimate; in some fields the last author denotes the advisor; revisit authorship as roles change [frassl2018].
- Frassl's rule 10: coauthorship implies substantial contribution, familiarity with the content and a check of its accuracy; all coauthors confirm their contribution and agree to the final text before every submission and resubmission [frassl2018].
- Zobel: authorship only for intellectual contribution; salami slicing and duplicate publication are misconduct (from notes) [zobel-writing-for-computer-science].

### The collaborative writing process

- Frassl's rule 2: the writing lead keeps the paper moving, communicates and acknowledges contributions, builds consensus, and still makes the hard calls on structure and content; the lead and first author are usually but not always the same person [frassl2018].
- Frassl's rule 5: everyone writing everything is rarely feasible; most papers are split into subsections by responsibility or expertise, or drafted by one principal writer, and the lead makes the final text cohesive [frassl2018].
- Frassl's rule 7: define the final deadline (external or by consensus), then intermediate milestones with tasks and time estimates; collaborative papers need more revision rounds than solo ones [frassl2018].
- Frassl's rule 8: be transparent about deadlines and expectations, name a note taker for decisions, and state the consequences of missing deadlines (changed author order, edits not included) [frassl2018].
- Frassl's rule 3: a data management plan, agreed by all coauthors, states how data and code are shared, versioned, stored and who may access them, and how the journal's deposition requirement will be met [frassl2018].

## Rules we adopt

1. Before any drafting, write the one-message sentence and a working title that states the contribution. Every outline item, figure and paragraph must serve that sentence or be cut. Checked by the paper-writing skill in the plan document. (from [mensh2017], [zhang2014], [schimel-writing-science])
2. Choose the audience and two or three candidate venues at the planning stage and record them in the plan; the venue decides the level of detail and the article type. Checked by the paper-writing skill against `assets/venues.md`. (from [zhang2014], [bourne2005], [romano2020])
3. Outline as one sentence per paragraph, starting from the results statements, and storyboard the figures before prose is written; the figures and their titles must tell the story alone. Checked by the paper-writing skill. (from [mensh2017], [zhang2014], [whitesides2004])
4. The abstract follows context, gap, approach, result, implication in that order, carries no citations, and states the result rather than promising one. Checked by `paper_lint.py`. (from [mensh2017], [zobel-writing-for-computer-science])
5. The introduction ends with a paragraph that states the contribution and how it is shown; it is not a literature review. Checked by `paper_lint.py`. (from [mensh2017], [zobel-writing-for-computer-science])
6. Each results subsection heading or figure title is a declarative statement; each results paragraph opens with its question and closes with its answer. Robert enforces in review; the skill checks captions. (from [mensh2017])
7. The discussion opens with a summary of the findings, gives each limitation its own paragraph, and ends with what the contribution enables; the conclusion, if separate, states what is open. Robert enforces in review. (from [mensh2017], [zobel-writing-for-computer-science])
8. Sections follow introduction, methods or results (venue order), discussion, conclusion; no section other than the introduction exceeds a third of the body. Checked by `paper_lint.py`. (from [mensh2017])
9. Authorship follows the four ICMJE criteria; contributions are recorded per author in the 14 CRediT roles at the planning stage and updated before submission; contributors who do not qualify are acknowledged. Checked by the paper-writing skill and `paper_lint.py` (statement present). (from [icmje], [credit], [frassl2018])
10. Every coauthor confirms contribution and approves the text before each submission and resubmission; the skill prepares the request, Robert sends it. (from [frassl2018])
11. Software papers cite an archived, versioned release with a DOI, keep usage examples out of the main text, and are written for users of the tool. Checked by the paper-writing skill and the code-audit skill. (from [romano2020])
12. Get at least one reading from someone outside the project before submission, and treat vague reviewer comments as a story problem rather than a wording problem. Robert enforces. (from [mensh2017], [bourne2005], [zhang2014])
13. The plan records the final deadline, intermediate milestones with owners, and who writes which section; a data management statement is agreed before the results are drafted. Checked by the paper-writing skill. (from [frassl2018])

## Where sources disagree

- Content first or context first: Mensh and Kording observe that news-style content-first structures serve impatient readers but risk skepticism when context is missing, and recommend context-content-conclusion as the default because the title and abstract already reveal the content [mensh2017]. Plaxco and Schimel push harder toward stating the finding early (from knowledge) [plaxco2010, schimel-writing-science]. We follow the default of Mensh and Kording for the body and put the finding in the title and the last sentence of the introduction.
- Thoroughness versus concision: Zhang's rule 5 asks for completeness with supporting data and his rule 6 for concision [zhang2014]; Bourne wants the most information in the fewest words [bourne2005]. We keep the main text to the argument and put completeness in supplements and repositories, which is also what reporting standards ask for.
- Methods position: Mensh and Kording assume methods are read least and place a methods summary in the first results paragraph [mensh2017]; computer science venues put methods before results [zobel-writing-for-computer-science]. We follow the venue and keep the first results paragraph as a methods summary in either case.
- Who qualifies as author: ICMJE requires all four criteria [icmje]; Frassl and colleagues report that groups differ on whether data provision qualifies and suggest being inclusive when in doubt [frassl2018]. We apply ICMJE and record the decision per person in the CRediT table so that the reasoning is visible.
