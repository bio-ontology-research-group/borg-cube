## What the evidence says

### Plan backward from the end date

- The final year needs a written plan of action, agreed between student and supervisor, with room for the unexpected; without one students risk being overwhelmed and missing deadlines [marino2014].
- One year before thesis submission is the point at which the student should decide how to spend the remaining months; the plan should be reviewed every few weeks to check progress [marino2014].
- The plan should rank priorities: the experiments that close the thesis or a final paper, mandatory administrative tasks, and time for the next career step [marino2014].
- Planning styles differ (timeline, mind map, checklist) and no one format is best; what matters is that a plan exists and is followed [marino2014, pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- The scope of the project should be defined early with the mentor, together with an explicit agreement on what must be completed for the degree; supervisors have an incentive to keep productive students longer, so the agreement protects the student [gu2007].
- Before committing to a project, check that it can reasonably be finished in the time allocated for graduation [gu2007].
- A time schedule and a list of deliverables (for example as a Gantt chart) keep a thesis project focused; poor planning is a known cause of wasted time and stalled progress [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- For computational work, estimate the compute time the analyses will need and feed that estimate into the schedule [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Students should collect the list of documents, credits and formatting requirements for submission before they start writing, and should not trust documents inherited from earlier students because rules change [marino2014].
- Funding often ends before the thesis does; the funding end date belongs in the plan a year ahead, and the student should discuss options with the supervisor early [marino2014].
- The doctorate is a fixed budget of years and should have an overall written plan with dated stages that is revised as the work moves (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd].
- The report recommends that every graduate student maintain an individual development plan that is revisited with mentors and that covers research milestones as well as career goals (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [nap2018-graduate-stem].

### The supervisor, regular meetings and the committee

- The supervisor should co-own the plan, review progress against it at intervals of a few weeks, and advise on thesis structure, chapter count and chapter length [marino2014].
- In a large group the principal investigator gives less direct mentoring, and other senior scientists become important; students should judge the whole environment, not only the head of the group [gu2007].
- Problems should be raised early with the mentor, whose role includes personal development as well as scientific guidance [gu2007].
- The thesis committee acts as a set of secondary mentors; students should talk to committee members between formal meetings and keep them informed, and the committee is valuable when student and primary mentor disagree [gu2007].
- Supervisors and committee members are far less familiar with the details of the project than the student; the student should update them periodically and run meetings with a pre-meeting agenda and post-meeting notes [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Good mentorship in computational work is less about hands-on coding help and more about building the student's judgment of what a sound analysis looks like [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- When a conflict with the supervisor cannot be resolved by discussion, the graduate school or academic committee can mediate [marino2014].
- Supervisors expect independence, non-first-draft written work, regular meetings and truthful progress reports; students expect their work to be read in advance, availability, constructive criticism and interest in their career. Most supervision failures are mismatches between these two lists (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd].
- The advisor relationship is the strongest single factor in whether a doctoral student finishes; many who left had no advisor or a poor match, and departments rarely knew which advisors lost students (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [lovitts2001].
- Students benefit from more than one mentor, and mentoring quality should be evaluated and rewarded by institutions rather than assumed (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [nap2018-graduate-stem].

### From coursework to independent research

- Research differs from taking courses; rotations and apprenticeships are the place to test aptitude and interest before committing to a project [gu2007].
- Independence should start as early as possible; the student should shape the project toward a distinct contribution rather than execute a given task [gu2007].
- Undergraduate training is structured and does not prepare students for failed experiments or for experiments that succeed without yielding anything useful [gu2007].
- Students lost in detail should return to the hypothesis and re-evaluate it periodically with their mentor [gu2007].
- Define the premise and boundaries of the study early and check that each methodological step advances the research goal; aims will still broaden or narrow as the work proceeds [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- At the start everything is slow, learning takes far longer than expected, and coding productivity is hard to measure, which makes loss of motivation easy; breaking the project into small goals and each goal into modules counters this [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Fear of starting is common; beginning with toy datasets and reproducing a published result at small scale moves a novice from theory to practice [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- The hard part of computational research is not running code but reflecting critically on results; students must learn to question output that looks too good [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- The transition from structured coursework to the unstructured dissertation stage is one of two attrition peaks; the other is the first year (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [lovitts2001].
- The doctorate passes through predictable psychological stages, including isolation after coursework and a long middle of frustration; naming the stage helps the supervisor act before it becomes a stall (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd].

### Write early and continuously

- A research project is never finished; at some point the student must decide that what exists is enough for a PhD and start writing, a stance summarized as "the truth can wait" [marino2014].
- A common mistake is to return to the lab for more data instead of starting the results chapters; postponing writing causes delays and rarely improves the thesis [marino2014].
- Computational students should start writing on day one; running further computations is a plausible excuse to avoid writing, which the authors call "procrastination by computation" [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Analysis should be punctuated with writing sessions whose output later becomes thesis material; drafting a literature review and anchoring each paper summary to a thesis position organizes reading [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Thesis writing can be reused: the literature review can become a review article and the introduction and outlook can seed a fellowship proposal [marino2014].
- Postdocs and collaborators can read chapters for content and flow; version control, a bibliography manager and a backup strategy protect the manuscript [marino2014].
- A student who has written nothing by the end of the first year is behind, whatever the lab work looks like; chapters go through many drafts and this takes longer than students expect (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd].

### How students fall behind

- The final year is stressful and isolating, and the writing process does not come naturally to everyone; students should enlist support from peers, postdocs, courses and family rather than face it alone [marino2014].
- Health comes first; hard work must be balanced with activities that give a break, or time is lost to illness [gu2007].
- Students misjudge their own computational skills; a skills gap discovered during the project slows research and damages motivation and self-esteem, so required skills and levels should be agreed with the supervisor before the project starts [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- If the required skills cannot be acquired within the time available, a different project is the wiser choice [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Thesis rings, code review meetings and peer meetings give feedback from more than one person, emotional support and better performance; group meetings, seminars and journal clubs expose students to how experienced scientists reason [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Peers, including non-coders, and informal communities are essential; shadowing an experienced practitioner is one of the fastest ways into a new field [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Doctoral attrition is not explained by ability: completers and leavers had similar entry credentials. Leavers were less integrated, knew fewer faculty, met their advisor less, and were less likely to have a desk in the department (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [lovitts2001].
- Departments assume the student who left was deficient, students blame themselves, and because nobody records departures the pattern stays invisible (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [lovitts2001].
- Programs should collect and publish completion, time-to-degree and career outcome data so that students and faculty can see the actual pattern (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [nap2018-graduate-stem].

### MSc thesis projects

- An MSc thesis is an educational activity of 6 to 12 months in which the student applies skills from coursework; the student should define personal learning goals before contacting a supervisor and check that the offered project matches them [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Reimplementing an existing validated tool to stay in a familiar language wastes time the project does not have, unless reimplementation is the goal [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Code and data should be managed FAIRly from day one, with an electronic notebook and a code repository, so that the work can be handed over or published after the student leaves [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Publication from an MSc thesis is possible but not guaranteed; the possibility, the time it needs after the thesis ends, and authorship should be agreed with the supervisor at the start [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Time for applications (PhD positions, internships) should be allocated in the thesis schedule so it does not silently delay the project [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].

### Interdisciplinary and computational pitfalls

- A student entering computational biology needs three foundations: domain concepts, technical proficiency in at least one language or environment, and the statistics relevant to the data; self-learning must be structured and workshops prioritized in discussion with advisors [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Mastering only one language biases how problems are seen; knowing more than one makes it easier to pick the right tool and to learn the next language [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis].
- Choose a widely used language at first for its documentation and community; anything repeated more than three times should become a script; heavy computation belongs on the cluster [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Before collecting data, set up a file management system and, if needed, a data management plan; every folder gets a readme, every research decision is written down, and results are kept reproducible from code [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Data curation is ruthless: large projects discard as much as 30 percent of the public data they evaluate, and unfit data confounds rather than helps [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Results are inferences, not truth; limitations should be recorded as the work proceeds, and pipelines should be tailored to the question rather than copied [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].
- Calibrate goals: "while you can do anything, you can't do everything" [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd].

### Career and leaving well

- Professional skills (writing, presenting, grant writing) should be developed alongside the thesis, and the next position should be lined up before graduation [gu2007].
- Fewer than 5 percent of PhD graduates reach a permanent academic position; students need both an academic plan and a viable alternative, and should use free institutional career support while enrolled [marino2014].
- Job applications can take as much time as a full-time job, and writing a thesis in evenings and weekends after starting a job is very hard [marino2014].
- Leaving on good terms means handing over code, data, protocols and unfinished projects, and agreeing on who finishes the last experiments and the order of authorship on outstanding papers [marino2014].
- PhD holders need a set of core competencies beyond research (communication, teamwork, ethics, project management), and programs should make career diversity visible and legitimate (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [nap2018-graduate-stem].

## Rules we adopt

1. Every doctoral student has a written, dated milestone plan built backward from the expected defense date, agreed with the supervisor in the first semester, and stored where both can read it (from [marino2014], [gu2007], [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd]).
2. Each milestone names an artefact: a document, a code release, a dataset, a submitted manuscript or a committee report. A milestone is complete only when the artefact exists (from [marino2014], [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis]).
3. The supervisor checks the plan at every 1:1 and the plan is formally revised at least once per semester, with the revision date recorded (from [marino2014], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd]).
4. A milestone whose date has passed by more than three months without its artefact is a flag the phd-milestones skill raises to the supervisor. The three-month threshold is a group choice; see Not covered (from [marino2014], [lovitts2001]).
5. Student and supervisor meet at least every two weeks, with a pre-meeting agenda and post-meeting notes. More than four weeks without a 1:1 is a flag (from [marino2014], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd]).
6. The scope of the thesis, meaning what is enough for the degree, is written down by the end of the first year of research and re-confirmed yearly. In the final year no aim is added without dropping one (from [gu2007], [marino2014]).
7. Writing starts in the first year: a literature review draft by the end of year one and at least one chapter or manuscript draft in every later year. A year without a written artefact is a flag (from [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd], [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd]).
8. The final-year plan is written at least twelve months before the defense and lists remaining experiments, chapters, administrative documents, the funding end date and career steps (from [marino2014]).
9. Within the first month, student and supervisor list the computational skills the project needs, the student's current level, and how the gap will be closed; the transition from coursework to research gets its own milestone (from [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd], [lovitts2001]).
10. The committee is formed early and receives a written progress update at least once per year outside formal examinations; students are encouraged to talk to members directly (from [gu2007], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd]).
11. Every student belongs to at least one peer structure (group meeting, code review, writing circle) and attendance is part of the plan (from [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis], [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd], [lovitts2001]).
12. When a student falls behind, the supervisor examines structure and integration first (meeting frequency, clarity of expectations, plan quality, desk and group contact) before questioning ability (from [lovitts2001], [gu2007]).
13. The group records milestone outcomes, delays and departures for every student and reviews them across students once per year (from [lovitts2001], [nap2018-graduate-stem]).
14. MSc thesis projects have a one-page plan before they start: learning goals, scope, deliverables with monthly dates, required skills, and a publication and authorship agreement (from [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis]).
15. Career planning enters the plan no later than eighteen months before the defense and is reviewed with the student like any other milestone (from [gu2007], [marino2014], [nap2018-graduate-stem]).

## Where sources disagree

- Format of the plan: [marino2014] and [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd] say planning styles vary and no format is best; [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis] recommends a schedule with deliverables such as a Gantt chart. We require a dated list of milestones with artefacts and leave the visual format free, because the skill needs dates and artefacts to track, and the sources agree that a plan must exist.
- Independence versus structure: [gu2007] stresses early independence and self-defined projects; [lovitts2001] and [nap2018-graduate-stem] stress structure, integration and mentoring as the drivers of completion. We structure the process (meetings, plan, milestones, peer groups) and keep the scientific content the student's own, since the attrition evidence concerns absent process, not absent freedom.
- When to start writing: [marino2014] frames writing as the step after the agreed experiments are done, in the final year; [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis] and [pcbi-2021-interdisciplinary-phd] want writing from day one. We follow continuous writing, and read [marino2014] as describing thesis assembly, which still has to be triggered by a deliberate "enough" decision.
- Committee timing: [gu2007] notes that some institutions convene the committee only near the end. We form it early because [gu2007] itself describes the committee as secondary mentors and [lovitts2001] shows that more faculty contact predicts completion.

## Not covered

- No source gives an evidence-based threshold for how long a milestone may slip before intervention. Our three-month and four-week flags are group choices to be tuned against our own records.
- No source gives a tested meeting frequency; [marino2014] says "every few weeks" for plan reviews and nothing more precise.
- The sources do not say how many milestones a doctorate should have or how to space qualifying exam, proposal defense and final defense; that is program-specific and left to the institutional file.
- Co-supervision and cross-institutional supervision are not treated.
- None of the sources says when a supervisor should advise a student to stop or convert to a master's degree, or how to make that conversation fair.
- Attrition data in [lovitts2001] come from two US universities in the 1990s; no source gives completion or time-to-degree figures for computational biology or for programs outside North America and Europe.
- Mental health beyond general advice on balance and support is not covered; the wellbeing file handles it.
- The effect of generative AI tools on thesis timelines is mentioned only for coding assistance in [pcbi-2025-msc-thesis]; nothing addresses its effect on writing, scope or assessment.
- Part-time and remote doctoral students are discussed in [phillips-pugh-how-to-get-a-phd] from knowledge only and not in any fetched source.
