# KAUST milestones for PhD and MS students

Sources
- kaust-cemse-milestones (KAUST page, proprietary; fetched 2026-09-02 from cemse.kaust.edu.sa/study/current-students/graduate-student-milestones-and-guidelines; quote rules short, never republish)
- kaust-registrar-program-guide (KAUST Program Guide 2025-2026, proprietary; fetched 2026-09-02: CS PhD, CS MS, Bioengineering PhD, Bioengineering MS and Time Limits and Extensions pages; quote rules short, never republish)
- kaust-graduate-affairs-thesis-policy (KAUST page, proprietary; not fetched, summarised from knowledge)
- kaust-proposal-format (KAUST rule, proprietary; not fetched, summarised from knowledge)

The manifest recorded only the domain roots for the first two entries. The
fetched pages are the CEMSE milestones page and the 2025-2026 Program Guide
pages for the two programmes the group supervises in (Computer Science in
CEMSE, Bioengineering in BESE). Rules differ by programme and by the semester
in which the student started, so every rule below names both.

## What the evidence says

### Which rule set applies

- KAUST runs two milestone timelines: one for students who started before Fall 2023 and one for students who started in Fall 2023 or later. The student's enrolment date decides which applies [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Under the pre-Fall-2023 rules the word semester includes Fall, Spring and Summer. Under the current rules the MS section states that semester means Fall and Spring only, and the Program Guide counts PhD duration as 8 Semesters and 4 Summer Sessions, so Summer sessions are counted separately [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- The CEMSE page is a reference; the Program Guide of the year of admission is the official statement of degree requirements [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- The Time Limits and Extensions policy in the 2025-2026 guide applies to students who enrolled from Fall 2023; earlier students fall under the previous policy [kaust-registrar-program-guide].

### PhD, students who started before Fall 2023

- Qualifying exam: PhD students must pass within 4 semesters; MS/PhD students within 7 semesters (semesters counted including Summer) [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Dissertation proposal defense: PhD students within 7 semesters; MS/PhD students within 11 semesters [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Degree completion: PhD within 5 years with a possible extension of up to 2 years; MS/PhD both degrees within 6 years with up to 1 year extension [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- One approved extension request equals one semester; a one-year extension needs three separate approvals by the Associate Dean on behalf of the Dean [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Missing a milestone deadline puts the student on probation for one semester; not completing it during probation leads to dismissal with an appeal to the Academic Performance Committee [kaust-cemse-milestones].

### PhD, students who started in Fall 2023 or later

- Qualifying exam by the end of the 3rd semester; MS/PhD students may complete it during the MS phase [kaust-cemse-milestones]. The CS guide phrases the same limit as three Semesters and one Summer Session after commencing the PhD [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Dissertation proposal defense by the end of the 5th semester after commencing the PhD; passing it confers PhD candidacy [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Complete the PhD within 4 years (8 Semesters and 4 Summer Sessions); extension up to 1 year, each approved extension being one semester or summer session, so a one-year extension needs three approvals [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- PhD students may apply for up to three extensions, each approved by the Dean before the start of the following semester or session, each with a statement of the remaining scope of work [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Failure to meet any milestone on time results in immediate dismissal with the option to appeal to the APC [kaust-cemse-milestones].

### The qualifier differs by programme

- Computer Science: the qualifier is course based. The student passes three eligible qualifier courses, one with A- or A and two with B+ or better, from three different research areas and taught by three different faculty. Courses taken during a KAUST MS may count; courses from outside KAUST are not waived. A filled qualifier form with transcript goes to the GPSA [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Bioengineering: the qualifier is the Pre-Proposal Committee Meeting, a research update judged on progress in the last academic year, milestones and plans for the next year, with a broad topic expected. The committee is the advisor, one other faculty from the programme and one from outside it; outcomes are pass, pass with conditions or fail [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Both programmes: outcomes are Pass, Fail with Retake (retake within the next period or by the milestone deadline, with possible probation or a study plan) and Fail Without Retake (unanimous committee vote endorsed by the Dean); the result form goes to the GPSA within two working days [kaust-registrar-program-guide].

### Proposal defense

- The petition and committee list go to the GPSA in one email two weeks before the defense; the written proposal goes to the committee at least 10 days before the presentation [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- CS: a proposal of 10 pages suggested length with topic, literature survey and preliminary results, then about 30 minutes of presentation and questions to a total of one hour [kaust-registrar-program-guide]. This matches the CEMSE-wide 10-page convention (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [kaust-proposal-format].
- Bioengineering: the proposal is under 30 pages or 12,000 words excluding references and must contain a literature review, the research problem and its novelty, methodology, preliminary results and a plan [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Proposal committee: three to four members; members 1 and 2 faculty with primary affiliation in the programme, member 3 faculty from outside the programme, member 4 optional (faculty or research scientist, inside or outside KAUST); the advisor chairs; Dean approval is required; proposal committee members must stay on the dissertation committee unless advisor and Dean approve otherwise [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Outcomes: Pass; Fail with Retake within three months or by the milestone deadline; Fail Without Retake ends the candidacy and needs a unanimous vote endorsed by the Dean. The result form is due within three days (CEMSE page) or two working days (guide) [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].

### Dissertation defense

- Petition by the academic-calendar deadline; Bioengineering adds at least two months before the defense date; the date needs the agreement of all committee members [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Dissertation committee: four to five members; members 1 to 3 as for the proposal, member 4 an external examiner from outside KAUST who writes a report, member 5 optional; visiting professors may not be the external examiner [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- The defense does not proceed until the advisor confirms receipt of the external examiner's report and approval of the final dissertation; the defense announcement (date, room, advisor, photo) goes to cemse@kaust.edu.sa at least two weeks before [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Outcomes: Pass with at most one dissenting vote; Pass with Conditions to be met within three months; Fail with Retake within six months; Fail Without Retake (unanimous) means dismissal [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- After the defense: result form to the GPSA within three calendar days (two days per the guide), Turnitin report and advisor approval, ORCID iD, and archiving in the KAUST Repository within 15 days of the defense (two weeks per the guide) or by the calendar deadline, whichever is first [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- The dissertation follows the Thesis and Dissertation Guidelines published by the KAUST Library [kaust-registrar-program-guide]. KAUST expects the dissertation to reach the committee about six weeks before the defense; the formatting template is a LaTeX class (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [kaust-graduate-affairs-thesis-policy].

### Coursework and standing

- PhD: minimum 72 credits overall, cumulative GPA 3.0, B- minimum per course, at least two 300-level courses (6 credits, one must be CS for CS students), graduate seminar (CS 398 two semesters; BioE 398 five semesters) and the Winter Enrichment Program once. Courses from a KAUST MS do not count toward PhD course requirements [kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- MS: minimum 36 credits, GPA 3.0, 12 credits of core courses; thesis track adds 12 credits of thesis research (CS 297 or BioE 297) [kaust-registrar-program-guide].

### MS timelines

- MS non-thesis: 3 semesters and 1 summer. MS thesis: 4 semesters and 1 summer for Fall starts; 3 semesters and 2 summers for Spring starts. One extension may be requested (a semester or a summer) [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- The thesis application must be approved by the Program Chair by the end of the first week of the 3rd semester; the thesis must be complete by the end of the 4th semester; students who cannot finish may drop to the non-thesis track until the end of the 3rd semester [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- MS thesis committee: three to four members with the same affiliation pattern as the proposal committee; the thesis goes to the committee at least three weeks before the defense [kaust-cemse-milestones].

## Rules we adopt

1. Compute every student's milestone deadlines from the start date, the programme (phd-cs, phd-bioeng, ms-cs, ms-bioeng), the track (thesis or not) and the rule set (pre-Fall-2023 or current); the phd-milestones script refuses to run without all of these (from [kaust-cemse-milestones], [kaust-registrar-program-guide]).
2. Count semesters as Fall and Spring for students under the current rules and as Fall, Spring and Summer for pre-Fall-2023 students; state the counting rule in every output (from [kaust-cemse-milestones]).
3. Treat the end of a semester as an approximate date and print a reminder that the Registrar's academic calendar holds the exact deadline; never present a computed date as the official one (from [kaust-cemse-milestones]).
4. Store the programme rules in `assets/programs.yaml` with the source id and the date the rule was verified against the fetched text; a rule without a verified-on date is printed with an unverified marker (from [kaust-registrar-program-guide]).
5. For each PhD milestone emit the internal preparation deadlines that precede it: committee petition two weeks before a proposal defense, proposal document ten days before, dissertation to the committee six weeks before, defense announcement two weeks before, result form within two working days, archiving within two weeks (from [kaust-cemse-milestones], [kaust-registrar-program-guide], [kaust-graduate-affairs-thesis-policy]).
6. Flag a milestone whose deadline is less than 60 days away when the org file shows no artefact (committee list, proposal draft, dissertation draft) for it; the 60-day window is a group choice, see Not covered (from [kaust-cemse-milestones]).
7. Flag any student whose computed degree deadline has passed without a recorded extension, because under the current rules a missed milestone means dismissal rather than probation (from [kaust-cemse-milestones]).
8. For MS-to-PhD transfers, start the PhD clock at the PhD commencement date and record which qualifier courses or the pre-proposal were completed during the MS, since the guide allows them to count (from [kaust-cemse-milestones], [kaust-registrar-program-guide]).
9. Check committee composition against the programme table before a petition is drafted: three or four members for proposals and MS theses, four or five for dissertations, with an external examiner from outside KAUST and no visiting professor as examiner (from [kaust-cemse-milestones], [kaust-registrar-program-guide]).
10. Re-fetch the CEMSE page and the Program Guide once per semester and diff them against the stored copies; any change becomes a bead for Robert before `programs.yaml` is edited (from [kaust-cemse-milestones]).

## Where sources disagree

- Result-form deadline after a defense: the CEMSE page says three calendar days, the Program Guide says two working days (two days for the dissertation form). We use two working days, the stricter of the two, and tell the student the calendar deadline overrides both [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Archiving deadline: the CEMSE page says within 15 days of the defense, the guide says two weeks from the final result form. We use 14 days from the defense as the internal target [kaust-cemse-milestones, kaust-registrar-program-guide].
- Bioengineering proposal length (under 30 pages or 12,000 words) differs from the CS 10-page suggestion; the script reports the programme's own limit and does not apply the CEMSE figure to BESE students [kaust-registrar-program-guide, kaust-proposal-format].
- The CS qualifier is a course-based check while staff.org and the Bioengineering programme speak of a pre-proposal; the milestone label in outputs is "qualifier" with the programme-specific form named in parentheses [kaust-registrar-program-guide].

## Not covered

- Exact semester start and end dates: they come from the Registrar's academic calendar, which was not fetched. The script uses fixed approximations that Robert should replace with calendar dates each year.
- The 60-day artefact window and what counts as an artefact are group choices; no KAUST document defines them.
- The pre-Fall-2023 rules for the MS/PhD combined track are stated only on the CEMSE page; the old Program Guide was not fetched.
- The external examiner selection guidelines sit behind KAUST login and were not fetched.
- The Graduate Affairs thesis formatting rules (template, front matter) were not fetched; the thesis-writing skill will need them.
