## What the evidence says

### How memory and understanding form

- How People Learn II describes learning as an active process in which
  learners build on prior knowledge, shaped by culture and context, and
  distinguishes types of learning (habit formation, observational,
  implicit pattern learning, perceptual and motor, and learning of facts,
  concepts and procedures) that follow different rules
  [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii].
- Memory is reconstructive: consolidation takes time and sleep, retrieval
  strengthens a memory more than re-exposure does, and spacing and
  interleaving practice improve long-term retention and transfer even
  though they feel harder [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii, make-it-stick].
- Ambrose and colleagues' seven principles: prior knowledge can help or
  hinder; how knowledge is organised governs how it is used; motivation
  directs and sustains effort; mastery needs component skills, their
  integration and knowing when to apply them; goal-directed practice with
  targeted feedback drives learning; climate affects learning; and learners
  must monitor and adjust their own approach [ambrose-how-learning-works].
- Discipline-based education research documents that students bring
  persistent misconceptions that instruction must surface and confront,
  that novices and experts differ in how they organise knowledge and read
  representations, and that metacognitive skill predicts success
  [nap2012-dber].
- Working memory holds only a few items at a time; the Carpentries teach
  this as the reason to chunk material, to build lessons around a concept
  map with few links per lesson, and to give novices a mental model before
  details [carpentries-instructor-training].
- Novices differ from experts in kind, not speed: experts retrieve stored
  patterns while novices assemble every step, so novice tasks must be
  small, self-contained and tool-supported [brown-wilson2018].

### Which study and practice techniques work

- Dunlosky and colleagues rated ten learning techniques: practice testing
  and distributed practice have high utility; interleaved practice,
  elaborative interrogation and self-explanation moderate utility;
  summarising, highlighting, keyword mnemonics, imagery for text and
  rereading low utility, although students favour the low-utility ones
  [dunlosky2013].
- Make It Stick popularises the same findings as desirable difficulties:
  retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, generation (attempting an
  answer before being shown it), elaboration and calibration against
  objective feedback; fluency from rereading is an illusion of knowing
  [make-it-stick].
- Deslauriers and colleagues show the illusion in class: the group taught
  by a fluent lecture felt it learned more and learned less than the group
  that struggled through active tasks [deslauriers2019].
- Freeman and colleagues' meta-analysis links classroom activity to higher
  exam scores and lower failure rates across science, engineering and
  mathematics [freeman2014].
- Worked examples with labelled subgoals, faded examples and Parsons
  problems lower the load of a task to what a novice can handle and improve
  transfer to new problems [brown-wilson2018, carpentries-instructor-training].
- Predicting the outcome of a demonstration before seeing it is what turns
  watching into learning [brown-wilson2018].

### Taxonomy of objectives

- The revised Bloom taxonomy separates the cognitive process (remember,
  understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create, each with sub-processes)
  from the knowledge type (factual, conceptual, procedural, metacognitive);
  an objective is a verb and a noun, and objectives, activities and
  assessments belong in the same cell [anderson-krathwohl2001].
- Retention needs remembering; transfer needs the other five processes;
  the hierarchy is loose and a cell describes a task, not a person
  [anderson-krathwohl2001].
- Metacognitive knowledge, knowing what one does not know and how one
  learns, is the signal of an independent learner and is taught, not
  assumed [anderson-krathwohl2001, nap2018-how-people-learn-ii].

### Multimedia and cognitive load

- Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning: words and pictures are
  processed in two limited channels and learning requires selecting,
  organising and integrating; design cuts extraneous processing, manages
  essential processing and fosters generative processing
  [mayer-multimedia-learning].
- Principles that cut extraneous load: coherence (drop decorative
  material), signalling (headings, arrows), redundancy (do not read text
  aloud that is also on screen), spatial and temporal contiguity (words
  next to and at the same time as the pictures they describe)
  [mayer-multimedia-learning, mayer-moreno2003].
- Principles that manage essential load: segmenting into learner-paced
  pieces, pre-training on the names of components, and the modality
  principle (spoken words with graphics beat printed words with graphics)
  [mayer-multimedia-learning, mayer-moreno2003].
- Mayer and Moreno's nine ways to reduce cognitive load include
  off-loading to the other channel, segmenting, pre-training, weeding,
  signalling, aligning, eliminating redundancy, synchronising and
  individualising [mayer-moreno2003].

### Motivation and belonging

- How People Learn II ties motivation to learners' goals, their belief
  that effort will pay off, their sense of belonging, and the value they
  see in the task; threats to identity and belonging depress performance
  independent of ability [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii].
- Instructor beliefs shape outcomes: believing that ability is innate
  leads to less investment in the students judged to lack it, and grade
  distributions in programming courses are not in fact bimodal
  [brown-wilson2018].
- Authentic tasks and early visible results raise persistence; the
  Carpentries pair this with avoiding demotivating language and with
  helping learners see the next attainable step
  [brown-wilson2018, carpentries-instructor-training].

## Rules we adopt

1. Every session and every course includes retrieval practice: low-stakes
   quizzes, questions answered before the answer is shown, cumulative
   problem sets; rereading and highlighting are never assigned as the study
   method. (from [dunlosky2013], [make-it-stick],
   [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii])
2. Space and interleave: topics reappear in later weeks' problems and
   exams are cumulative; the week plan shows where each outcome is
   revisited. Checked by the course-design skill. (from [dunlosky2013],
   [make-it-stick])
3. Start every unit with a probe of prior knowledge and design the first
   activity to surface the likely misconception. (from
   [ambrose-how-learning-works], [nap2012-dber])
4. Limit each lesson to a few new concepts drawn from a concept map, give a
   mental model before details, and use worked examples with labelled
   subgoals before independent practice. (from
   [carpentries-instructor-training], [brown-wilson2018])
5. Every objective is a verb plus a noun with a Bloom level and knowledge
   type, and the assessment sits in the same cell. Checked by the
   alignment matrix. (from [anderson-krathwohl2001])
6. Slides and handouts follow the multimedia principles: no decorative
   material, no reading text that is on the screen, labels next to the
   figure element they name, one idea per slide, technical terms introduced
   before the explanation that uses them. Checked by the presentation
   skill. (from [mayer-multimedia-learning], [mayer-moreno2003])
7. Tell students how learning works: why active tasks feel harder, why
   fluency misleads, and how to study, in week one and in the syllabus.
   (from [deslauriers2019], [make-it-stick])
8. Feedback is targeted at the goal, timely and followed by a chance to
   act on it; a grade without a next step is not feedback. (from
   [ambrose-how-learning-works], [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii])
9. Build metacognition into the assessment: self-assessment against the
   published specification before submission, and a short reflection on
   what to change next time. (from [anderson-krathwohl2001],
   [ambrose-how-learning-works])
10. Never label a student's ability; describe the task level reached, with
    the evidence and date. (from [anderson-krathwohl2001],
    [brown-wilson2018])

## Where sources disagree

- Effort and enjoyment: Dunlosky, Make It Stick and Deslauriers show that
  the techniques that work feel worse [dunlosky2013, make-it-stick,
  deslauriers2019]; How People Learn II and the Carpentries stress
  motivation and belonging [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii,
  carpentries-instructor-training]. We keep the difficulty in the task and
  remove it from the climate: hard problems, explained purpose, early
  wins, no dismissive language.
- Modality: Mayer's modality principle prefers narration over on-screen
  text for fast-paced material [mayer-multimedia-learning]; self-paced
  handouts and reference material are text. We narrate in class and publish
  text for after class, and never do both at once on the same slide.
- Hierarchy: Bloom's process order is loose [anderson-krathwohl2001] and
  How People Learn II describes types of learning that do not order at all
  [nap2018-how-people-learn-ii]. We use the Bloom order only to compare the
  demanded level of tasks, not as a sequence for teaching.
- Evidence base: the meta-analyses and reports are undergraduate
  [freeman2014, nap2012-dber]; laboratory findings on spacing and retrieval
  are broader [dunlosky2013]. We apply both to graduate teaching and
  measure with our own before-and-after probes.

## Not covered

- Learning in research apprenticeship (a PhD) as opposed to courses; the
  sources address classrooms and workshops.
- Assessment of transfer beyond the course; none of the sources gives a
  practical measure we could run.
- Language: most of our students learn in a second language; the sources
  do not address the extra load, beyond Mayer's advice to pre-train
  vocabulary.
- Generative AI as a study aid or as a source of illusions of knowing.
- How People Learn II, the DBER report, Dunlosky 2013, Mayer and Moreno
  2003, Ambrose and Make It Stick were not fetched or have no notes file;
  claims here come from memory and need checking before a skill quotes
  them.
