## What the evidence says

### Why the document exists at all

- Masters and Kreeger separate two documents that are often confused: a
  development plan works on the mentee's long-term goals, and an expectations
  document covers the day-to-day operation of the lab, which they identify as
  the source of many conflicts for both sides [masters2017].
- Their first rule is to write it down, because a written document
  communicates expectations more consistently than conversation, lets both
  sides revisit them as the relationship develops, and gives a record if
  either side does not follow them [masters2017].
- They add that even an incomplete draft is worth having, and that a
  prospective mentee can use the document to judge whether the group suits
  them before joining [masters2017].
- Barker makes the same argument at group level: a lab without stated rules
  still has rules, they are just unspoken and inconsistent, and a new member
  should know within their first weeks what is expected about hours,
  notebooks and data, authorship, presenting, and how they will hear from the
  head of the group [barker-at-the-helm].
- Delamont and colleagues report that supervisors' expectations are largely
  tacit and that the early months set the pattern, and recommend scheduled
  supervisions from the first week, a written record of each meeting with what
  was agreed and by when, and an early piece of written work
  [delamont-supervising-the-doctorate].

### What goes in it

- Tailor by role: postdocs, graduate students, undergraduates and staff have
  different needs, so separate versions are useful; the postdoc version
  emphasises leadership and independence [masters2017].
- Convey the big picture, meaning the group's culture and the supervisor's
  mentoring philosophy, so the mentee can judge the fit [masters2017].
- Do not stop there: state the concrete operating rules. Their own document
  covers hours and vacation, safety and shared jobs, conflict resolution and
  how authorship is decided; for computational work they name policies on
  working remotely and documentation requirements for code [masters2017].
- Expectations run both ways. The document states what the mentee can expect
  of the mentor, because a mentee who wants regular feedback and a mentor who
  works hands-off will otherwise collide [masters2017].
- Articulate boundaries with the power difference in mind: professional
  boundaries such as whether the mentee is expected to help with the mentor's
  own work, and personal boundaries such as how fast questions are answered
  and when a personal phone number may be used [masters2017].
- Everything in it must be consistent with the rules of the institution and
  the programme [masters2017]. For KAUST students, the programme milestones,
  committee composition and degree deadlines are set by the division and the
  registrar, not by the group [kaust-cemse-milestones].
- Wisker's learning contract covers roles, meeting frequency, how drafts are
  exchanged, turnaround times, what the supervisor will and will not do, and
  how disagreements are handled [wisker-good-supervisor].
- Healthy-lab advice adds explicit attention to workload, working hours and
  recognition, and treats a lab where credit is unclear as a predictable
  source of conflict [maestre2019].
- Data and record-keeping rules belong in the same document: where records
  live, who can read them, what state a project is in, and what happens to the
  records when a person leaves [pcbi-2023-lab-information].

### The writing partnership needs its own paragraphs

- Quynn and colleagues put expectation setting for shared writing at the start
  of each project: roles, resources, authorship, how and when the two people
  communicate, how drafts and feedback are exchanged, what tools are used, and
  how long the process realistically takes [quynn2026].
- They give generative AI its own rule, arguing that the risks and the
  potential are large enough that its role must be settled explicitly, and
  noting that the student may have more experience with these tools than the
  advisor [quynn2026].
- Feedback type is agreed in advance: early drafts benefit from questions and
  comments about framing and organisation, later drafts from corrective
  feedback on language and concision, and both sides should say which stage a
  draft is at when they hand it over [quynn2026].
- They warn that wholesale rewriting by the mentor gets the document out of
  the door but removes the learning, and that the mentee accepting all changes
  without reading them has the same effect [quynn2026].
- Low-stakes assignments early (outlines, README files, figure legends,
  methods sections) let the mentee practice receiving feedback before the
  stakes are high [quynn2026].
- Time is the recurring failure: several rounds of feedback per section are
  normal, people consistently underestimate how long writing takes, and the
  timeline should be agreed rather than assumed [quynn2026].

### Authorship

- The ICMJE authorship criteria require substantial contribution to the work,
  involvement in drafting or revising it critically, approval of the final
  version, and agreement to be accountable for it; contributors who do not
  meet all four are acknowledged rather than listed as authors. The fetched
  page is only a table of contents, so this entry is from general knowledge
  [icmje].
- CRediT is a standard vocabulary of contributor roles (conceptualization,
  data curation, formal analysis, funding acquisition, investigation,
  methodology, project administration, resources, software, supervision,
  validation, visualization, writing of the original draft, and writing
  through review and editing) that lets a contribution be recorded precisely
  instead of argued about by author position [credit].
- Masters and Kreeger list how authorship is determined as one of the
  concrete rules the document should state in advance [masters2017].

### Keeping it alive

- Get input from others: discussing a draft with colleagues, one's own
  mentors, or senior mentees improves it, and Masters and Kreeger describe a
  small writing group working through several meetings as an effective way to
  finish one [masters2017].
- Copying is encouraged with permission, but only where the copied statement
  actually reflects how you mentor, since a statement that does not sends
  mixed messages [masters2017].
- Understanding of the document is theoretical when a mentee joins and becomes
  concrete as they see it applied, so regular conversation about how it is
  interpreted heads off misunderstandings; setting aside part of one group
  meeting a year for this is one way [masters2017].
- It is a living document: start small and refine over time, since unforeseen
  situations arise and programme or institutional rules change
  [masters2017].
- The mentoring philosophy assignment in Entering Mentoring plays the same
  role from the supervisor's side: writing down how you mentor and why makes
  the practice something that can be revisited and held to [handelsman2005].
- The National Academies stress that a written document does not by itself
  make a relationship work; it is the conversation and the return to it that
  do [nap2019-mentorship].

## Rules we adopt

1. Draft the compact before the first onboarding meeting and never deliver it
   as a finished agreement. The system fills only what the sources and Robert's
   stated pattern support and leaves the student's side blank for the meeting.
   Checked by the mentoring-compact skill. (from [masters2017],
   [nap2019-mentorship])
2. Every commitment in the compact carries a number or a date: meeting
   cadence, reply time for short questions, feedback turnaround for a section
   and for a full manuscript, notice period for cancelling a meeting. A draft
   with a vague commitment is not ready. Checked by the mentoring-compact
   skill. (from [masters2017], [wisker-good-supervisor], [quynn2026])
3. Cover both directions. For every expectation of the student there is a
   matching statement of what the student can expect of Robert, including what
   he will not do. Checked by the mentoring-compact skill. (from
   [masters2017], [wisker-good-supervisor])
4. Name a conflict path that does not run through Robert (graduate affairs,
   the ombuds, a second supervisor), because the power difference makes the
   dyad an unsafe sole channel. Checked by the mentoring-compact skill.
   (from [masters2017], [nap2019-mentorship])
5. Settle authorship at the outline stage of each paper, record it with CRediT
   roles, and apply the ICMJE criteria to decide who is an author rather than
   author order habits. The compact states this rule; the paper records the
   outcome. (from [icmje], [credit], [masters2017])
6. State the role of generative AI in writing and coding explicitly: what it
   may be used for, what must be disclosed, and that the student remains
   responsible for every claim. (from [quynn2026])
7. State the writing process: which stage a draft is at when it is sent, what
   kind of feedback is wanted, how many rounds to expect, and the turnaround
   at each round. Robert comments rather than rewrites, except on a final pass
   agreed as such. (from [quynn2026])
8. Keep operating rules (hours, presence, leave, storage, notebooks, code
   location, safety, teaching load) in the compact and career direction in the
   IDP, and never mix assessment into either. (from [masters2017],
   [pcbi-2023-lab-information], [maestre2019])
9. Say in the document itself that KAUST policy and the programme rules take
   precedence wherever they differ, and check every draft against the
   milestone plan before it goes to Robert. Checked by the mentoring-compact
   skill. (from [masters2017], [kaust-cemse-milestones])
10. Maintain a role-specific version: the postdoc addendum covers the
    contract, the independence path, senior authorship and the exit; it is
    attached rather than merged, so the student version stays short.
    (from [masters2017])
11. Revisit the compact on a fixed twelve-month cycle and whenever the
    project, funding or role changes, add a dated version line for every
    change, and keep every version so the diff is possible.
    (from [masters2017], [delamont-supervising-the-doctorate])
12. Ask the student twice a year what the supervisor should do differently and
    record the answer in the next version, so the document is revised from
    both sides. (from [masters2017], [wisker-good-supervisor])

## Where sources disagree

- Detail versus principle: the AAMC-style compacts Masters and Kreeger
  describe stay at the level of general principles, and they argue that this
  is exactly what fails, because the conflicts come from the day-to-day rules
  [masters2017]. The National Academies stay at the relationship level
  [nap2019-mentorship]. We follow Masters and Kreeger and write the concrete
  rules, while keeping the document short enough to be read.
- Copying existing text: Masters and Kreeger explicitly encourage reusing
  wording that resonates [masters2017]; Handelsman's mentoring philosophy
  assignment insists the statement be the mentor's own [handelsman2005]. We
  reuse structure and boilerplate, and require Robert to write the philosophy
  and the feedback commitments himself.
- Who owns the writing timeline: Quynn and colleagues put the schedule in a
  shared agreement made per project [quynn2026]; Delamont treats the timetable
  as supervisor-managed across the whole registration
  [delamont-supervising-the-doctorate]. We keep the standing turnaround times
  in the compact and agree a per-paper timeline at the outline stage.

## Not covered

- What to do when the agreed expectations are broken. The sources treat the
  document as a reference point for conversation, and none gives an
  escalation procedure short of institutional processes.
- KAUST-specific employment terms for postdocs (contract length, notice,
  visa consequences of non-renewal), which are set by human resources and are
  not in any fetched source.
- How to write a compact for a student co-supervised across institutions, or
  for a visiting student on someone else's funding.
- Whether written expectations change outcomes. The claim in the sources is
  about reduced conflict and clearer communication, not about completion or
  productivity.
- The ICMJE entry here is from general knowledge because only the site's
  table of contents was fetched; verify the four criteria against the
  recommendations before quoting them anywhere.
