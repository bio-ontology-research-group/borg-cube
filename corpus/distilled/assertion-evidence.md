The local `presentation` skill (~/Public/software/skills/local/presentation/SKILL.md)
remains the authority on Robert's slide rules: statement titles, one message per
slide, define or drop, no forward references, no meta labels, sentence case, no
em-dashes, figure captions verified against pixels. This file collects the
evidence behind those rules so that a talk-design agent can explain and defend
them, and so that the rules can be updated when the evidence changes.

## What the evidence says

### The structure: a sentence assertion supported by visual evidence

- The assertion-evidence approach replaces the software default of a phrase headline over a bulleted list with a headline that states the slide's message as a full sentence, supported by visual evidence (figure, diagram, image, equation, table) rather than text [alley-assertion-evidence].
- Phrase headlines such as "Results" or "Method" do not communicate; a message headline tells the audience what to take from the slide before the speaker says a word [alley-assertion-evidence, naegle2021].
- The slide title should be the message or conclusion, phrased like the first or last sentence of a paragraph; everything on the slide is support for that sentence, and the audience should arrive at the title's conclusion after hearing the slide discussed [naegle2021].
- Bulleted lists create noise: the audience does not know where to look, and the text competes with the speaker; a slide should rarely be text only and should be built around a strong visual [alley-assertion-evidence, naegle2021].
- The headline is a complete sentence in no more than two lines, left-justified, in large sans-serif type (about 28 point); body text stays at 18 to 24 point and is limited to labels and short callouts (from knowledge of the book, see notes) [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations].
- Headings, slide titles and captions should be statements that can be true or false, not topics; the body proves the statement. Doumont calls this the theorem rule and applies it to documents and talks alike [doumont-trees-maps-theorems].
- Bullet outlines remove the connective words that carry an argument (because, therefore, unless), and a critical finding buried at the fourth level of a bullet hierarchy under a reassuring title can be missed entirely [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint].

### Evidence that it works

- In a controlled study two audiences heard the same spoken content with different slides; the audience shown assertion-evidence slides scored significantly higher on comprehension and retention than the audience shown topic-phrase headlines with bullets (p below 0.01) [alley-assertion-evidence, garner-alley2013].
- Garner and Alley attribute the gain to the sentence headline, which orients the audience and reduces the effort of working out the point, and to visual evidence, which uses the pictorial channel instead of competing with speech (from knowledge of the source, not verified against the text) [garner-alley2013].
- A follow-up study found that student presenters who built assertion-evidence slides showed deeper understanding of their own content than presenters who built topic-subtopic slides; writing the assertion forces the speaker to know the point [alley-assertion-evidence].
- Pilot studies from 2023 report the approach also helps expert audiences, warn that decorative images and generated diagram art can backfire, and recommend paraphrasing long quotations rather than displaying them [alley-assertion-evidence].
- Full sentences on slides overload the audience because reading and listening share one channel; images and video use a separate channel and can coexist with speech; text should be sparse guideposts rather than prose [naegle2021].
- Text that is brief and redundant with what is said improves retention, whereas long text that must be read does not [naegle2021].

### One idea per slide, one minute per slide

- Each slide carries one central message; complex diagrams are introduced in stages across slides rather than shown complete at once [naegle2021].
- Plan about one minute per slide, so a 20-minute talk has about 20 slides; a slide that takes longer in rehearsal has too much content and must be split or cut [naegle2021].
- Visuals should appear at about one per minute of speaking and provide evidence for what is said rather than duplicate it; the speaker should not read them aloud [bourne2007-oral].
- Less is more: a clear main message that leaves room for questions beats a dense talk, and a talk that draws no questions was either incomprehensible or trivial [bourne2007-oral].
- Every element on a slide must be discussed; anything not important enough to talk about is removed, because committees and senior audiences will chase any stray detail [naegle2021].
- Design each slide so that a distracted listener still gets the main point from the slide alone; detail belongs in a backup slide after the acknowledgments for use during questions [naegle2021].
- Phillips's threshold, cited by Naegle: more than six elements on a slide raises the cognitive effort sharply, so keep slides to six or fewer elements [naegle2021].

### Structure and orientation of the talk

- A talk is a story with a beginning, middle and end: set the stage, tell the story, and finish with a memorable close in which the take-home message is unmistakable [bourne2007-oral].
- The audience should remember three points a week later; if they remember three random points instead of the intended ones, the emphasis was wrong [bourne2007-oral].
- A mapping slide previews the main sections in order, transitions signpost them, and the conclusion restates the main assertions; the audience should always know where they are (from knowledge of the book, see notes) [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations].
- Open by answering why the audience should listen (context, need, task, object of the talk); state the message before the details; close by restating the messages and saying what should happen next [doumont-trees-maps-theorems].
- For a dissertation defense, present only a slice of the work at a pace the committee can follow, and say plainly that the slice is a fraction of the dissertation; a fishbone or overview diagram places the slice in the whole [alley-assertion-evidence].
- Credits and citations are the exception to the cut-everything rule: add them when the slide is made, in a consistent format that distinguishes them from content, and use them to make clear who did what [naegle2021, bourne2007-oral].

### Posters as the same structure on paper

- Decide what the poster should make the viewer do (discuss, try the method, collaborate) before designing it, and sell the work in ten seconds with a compelling question answered clearly [erren-bourne2007].
- The title works like a newspaper headline: short, sharp, posing a decisive question or hinting at a novel finding, in language a broad audience can read [erren-bourne2007].
- The poster follows an abstract's logic (motivation, hypothesis, main results, conclusions), with conclusions placed prominently, and uses white space, arrows or numbering to guide the eye; type is at least 24 point [erren-bourne2007].
- Figures should work at two levels, a bold trend readable from a distance and detail on close reading; text is concise and graphics do the explaining [erren-bourne2007].
- Poster impact happens during and after the session: engage visitors, show contact details, hand out the paper or the poster, and collect follow-ups; visitors remember the person more than the poster [erren-bourne2007].

### Where Robert's rules come from

- Statement titles and one message per slide are the assertion-evidence headline rule and Naegle's rules 1 and 3 [alley-assertion-evidence, naegle2021, doumont-trees-maps-theorems].
- Define or drop and no forward references follow from the one-channel argument: an undefined symbol forces the audience to read and reconstruct while the speaker talks [naegle2021, alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations].
- No meta labels and no filler are the removal of noise that carries no message [doumont-trees-maps-theorems, tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint].
- Bullets over boxes, and plain nested bullets only where a visual is impossible, reconcile the assertion-evidence ban on bullet bodies with the reality of technical content; the sentence headline still does the communicating [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations, naegle2021].
- Figures carry their own explanation, and a caption may claim only what the pixels show, because a title that reassures while the evidence says otherwise is the failure mode Tufte documents [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint, naegle2021].

## Rules we adopt

1. Every content slide has a title that is a complete sentence stating the slide's message; titles that are topics or questions fail slide_lint (from [alley-assertion-evidence], [garner-alley2013], [naegle2021], [doumont-trees-maps-theorems]).
2. One message per slide and about one minute per slide: the outline lists the message for each slide and the slide count is within 20 percent of the minutes available (from [naegle2021], [bourne2007-oral]).
3. The body of a slide is evidence for its title: a figure, diagram, table, equation or image. Bulleted text is allowed only where no visual exists, and then as plain nested bullets with at most six elements (from [alley-assertion-evidence], [naegle2021], [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations]).
4. No sentence appears on a slide that the speaker will say aloud; on-slide words are labels, the title, and short guideposts (from [naegle2021], [garner-alley2013]).
5. Every symbol, metric, baseline and acronym is defined on or before the slide that first shows it; a slide that needs an experiment introduces it (from [naegle2021], [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations]).
6. The talk has a mapping slide or an equivalent explicit structure, and the closing slide restates the three take-home messages with the citation and repository (from [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations], [doumont-trees-maps-theorems], [bourne2007-oral]).
7. Every element on a slide is discussed; anything not discussed is cut or moved to a backup slide after the acknowledgments (from [naegle2021]).
8. Titles state what the evidence shows, including negative results; a reassuring title over contrary evidence is a review-blocking defect (from [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint], [naegle2021]).
9. Posters use the same rule: a headline that states the finding or poses the decisive question, an abstract-shaped layout with prominent conclusions, type of at least 24 point, and contact details (from [erren-bourne2007]).
10. Where this file and the local presentation skill differ, the presentation skill wins; changes to Robert's rules are made there and the evidence is updated here (group rule).

## Where sources disagree

- Bullets: [alley-assertion-evidence] and [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint] reject bulleted bodies outright; [naegle2021] allows sparse text as guideposts; Robert's presentation skill prefers plain nested bullets to colored blocks and wide tables for structured technical content. We follow the presentation skill: a sentence title always, visual evidence where one exists, plain bullets as the fallback, never boxes.
- Slides as handouts: [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint] wants a dense printed handout and few slides; [alley-assertion-evidence] answers the handout need with notes pages exported to PDF; [naegle2021] wants a PDF of the slides for robustness. We keep the deck as the audience artefact, export PDF, and point to the paper for detail.
- Speaker photo and personality: [erren-bourne2007] recommends a photograph and personal touches on a poster; the slide sources say nothing. We allow it on posters and not on talk slides.
- Density for expert audiences: the 2023 pilots on [alley-assertion-evidence] say the approach also helps experts; Robert's practice accepts denser slides for a specialist committee. We keep the sentence title everywhere and relax only the element count for committee talks.

## Not covered

- None of the sources gives an evidence-based slide count for talks longer than an hour, for lectures, or for online talks with screen sharing.
- The comprehension studies used engineering undergraduates; no source tests the approach on computational biology conference audiences or on non-native English listeners.
- No source addresses live coding, notebook demonstrations or animated figures in scientific talks.
- The sources do not treat title slides, acknowledgment slides or venue templates that force a logo bar on every slide.
- Beamer mechanics (statement titles longer than one line in a given theme, overfull boxes) are handled by the presentation skill, not by the evidence.
