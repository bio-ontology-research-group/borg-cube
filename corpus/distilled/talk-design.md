## What the evidence says

### Argument and structure

- The assertion-evidence approach replaces phrase headlines and bullet lists
  with succinct message headlines supported by visual evidence, because topic
  labels do not state the takeaway and bullet lists create visual clutter.
  [alley-assertion-evidence]
- A slide needs one central idea, and a speaker who takes more than about one
  minute to explain it should split or remove material. [naegle2021]
- A slide heading can state the message as a conclusion, allowing the slide
  body to support the same conclusion rather than act as a container called
  "Results" or "Methods." [naegle2021]
- A talk should fit its target audience, preserve a small number of lasting
  take-home points, follow a logical beginning, middle, and end, and finish
  with an unmistakable take-home message. [bourne2007-oral]
- The book notes describe a map at the start, section signposts, and a closing
  return to the main assertions as the devices that make a talk's hierarchy
  visible to listeners. [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations,
  doumont-trees-maps-theorems]
- In Doumont's formulation, a message is a proposition that can be true or
  false, and the visual material should prove the message rather than leave
  the audience to infer it. [doumont-trees-maps-theorems]
- Tufte's book notes warn that bullet hierarchies hide causal and comparative
  relations, and that an important finding can be buried under a reassuring
  title. [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint]

### Evidence and audience processing

- Visuals should be introduced and explained as evidence; complex or
  multipanel figures usually need to be reduced to the part that carries the
  slide's one idea. [naegle2021]
- The multimedia-learning notes describe separate visual and verbal channels
  with limited capacity, supporting sparse text, signaling, and labels close
  to the visual element they explain. [mayer-multimedia-learning]
- The same notes distinguish useful redundancy across channels from projecting
  the prose that the speaker says, which competes with listening. [mayer-multimedia-learning]
- Naegle recommends giving figure and data credit at the time a slide is made,
  using a consistent attribution style so credit remains visible and separate
  from the main message. [naegle2021]
- The local record has no fetched text or notes for the Garner and Alley study,
  so its result must not be used as a factual basis for this skill until the
  article is obtained and read. [garner-alley2013]
- The local record has no fetched text or notes for Mayer and Moreno's 2003
  article, so claims specific to its nine methods require verification against
  the original before they are adopted. [mayer-moreno2003]

### Rehearsal and review

- Practice helps a speaker stay on the planned content and avoid tangents;
  important talks should first be given to collaborators or peers who can find
  discrepancies. [bourne2007-oral]
- Rehearsal tests whether the intended titles and guideposts recur on every
  run, whether transitions connect, and whether a slide contains too much,
  nonessential detail, or a missing reference. [naegle2021]
- The book notes recommend standing, speaking aloud with the real slides and a
  timer, and separately rehearsing the first minute and transitions. [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations]
- Recording and reviewing a presentation makes violations easier to observe;
  timing should include acknowledgments and questions rather than treating
  them as free time. [bourne2007-oral]
- Poster guidance differs by medium but shares the need for purpose, a quick
  compelling title, concise content, guided reading order, and visual evidence
  that works at a distance. [erren-bourne2007]

## Rules we adopt

1. State the audience question and three take-home points before planning
   slides. Remove a section that does not support one of those points. (from
   [bourne2007-oral], [doumont-trees-maps-theorems])
2. Make an assertion-evidence outline before a slide plan: every section has
   an audience question, a sentence answer, evidence, significance, and a
   transition. (from [alley-assertion-evidence],
   [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations])
3. Give every content slide one complete-sentence assertion and only visual or
   verbal material that supports it. Split a slide that cannot be explained in
   about one minute. (from [naegle2021], [doumont-trees-maps-theorems])
4. Make the argument's tree visible with a map near the opening, signposted
   sections, and a closing return to the asserted take-homes. (from
   [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations], [doumont-trees-maps-theorems])
5. Define a metric, method, dataset, baseline, acronym, or experiment before
   showing a result that depends on it. Put the mechanism before the headline
   number. (from [naegle2021], [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint])
6. Put a source and a caption or spoken explanation with every borrowed or
   data-bearing figure. The caption says what varies, what is measured, and
   what the audience should see. (from [naegle2021],
   [mayer-multimedia-learning])
7. Use short guideposts, labels beside the visual elements they identify, and
   spoken explanation instead of a projected transcript of the speech. (from
   [mayer-multimedia-learning], [naegle2021])
8. Rehearse the real deck standing and aloud with a timer before an important
   talk. Include transitions, the first minute, acknowledgments, and questions
   in the practice plan. (from [bourne2007-oral],
   [alley-craft-of-scientific-presentations])
9. Review rendered slides and their underlying paper, figure, and code before
   commenting. Write one slide-numbered problem and fix at a time, resolving
   structural failures before wording. (from [naegle2021],
   [doumont-trees-maps-theorems])
10. If a timed run exceeds its slot by more than ten percent, cut or split
    material and rehearse again. Do not solve the problem by speaking faster.
    (from [bourne2007-oral], [naegle2021])

## Where sources disagree

- Slide density: Naegle favors one focused idea and about one minute per slide
  [naegle2021], while Tufte argues that projected material often loses needed
  comparisons when it is split into sparse slides [tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint].
  We keep one assertion per slide but allow a dense comparison figure when the
  comparison itself is the single assertion; detailed material belongs in a
  handout or paper. [naegle2021, tufte-cognitive-style-powerpoint]
- Redundant words: the multimedia-learning notes favor narration plus graphics
  over narration, graphics, and the same prose on screen
  [mayer-multimedia-learning], while Naegle allows short redundant guideposts
  [naegle2021]. We allow the assertion title, labels, and short guideposts,
  but not body text that duplicates the speech. [mayer-multimedia-learning,
  naegle2021]
- Delivery style: Bourne invites speakers to treat the floor as a stage when it
  fits them [bourne2007-oral], while the Doumont notes define non-message
  material as noise [doumont-trees-maps-theorems]. We allow an anecdote or
  demonstration only when it carries the argument and suits the speaker.
  [bourne2007-oral, doumont-trees-maps-theorems]

## Not covered

- These sources do not establish a universal slide count, because audience,
  material, and talk setting change the time needed for evidence. [naegle2021,
  bourne2007-oral]
- The sources do not give a controlled comparison of slide-numbered written
  reviews and oral practice feedback. [naegle2021, bourne2007-oral]
- Online, hybrid, and pre-recorded talks are not sufficiently addressed here;
  apply the same evidence and rehearsal checks, then judge platform-specific
  choices in context. [naegle2021, bourne2007-oral]
- The original Garner and Alley study and Mayer and Moreno article need local
  source text before their detailed findings can refine these rules.
  [garner-alley2013, mayer-moreno2003]
