# Systematic Approaches to a Successful Literature Review

Source: Booth A, Sutton A, Papaioannou D (2016) Systematic Approaches to a Successful Literature Review, 2nd ed. SAGE
Status: drafted from general knowledge of the work on 2026-09-02; cite-only book; Robert to review and correct.

## Main ideas

- A textbook by three information specialists for students and researchers
  who must review literature. Its thesis: every review, including a thesis
  chapter or the background of a grant, gains from being systematic in the
  sense of explicit, transparent and repeatable, even when it is not a full
  systematic review in the Cochrane sense.
- The review is a staged project: planning and scoping, defining the
  question, searching, assessing the evidence, synthesizing, analyzing,
  writing up, with project management (time, references, records) running
  across all stages.
- Review types are characterized with the SALSA framework (search, appraisal,
  synthesis, analysis) taken from Grant and Booth; a chapter helps the reader
  choose between scoping, mapping, rapid, systematic, realist, qualitative,
  mixed-method and umbrella reviews according to purpose and resources.
- Question formulation uses frameworks: PICO or PICOC (population,
  intervention, comparison, outcome, context), SPICE (setting, perspective,
  intervention, comparison, evaluation), SPIDER for qualitative questions.
  A structured question yields the concept blocks that the search is built
  from.
- Searching: a scoping search first, to size the literature and find existing
  reviews; balance sensitivity (recall) against specificity (precision);
  combine synonyms with OR inside a concept block and blocks with AND;
  controlled vocabulary (MeSH) plus free text; truncation and wildcards;
  translate the strategy across database interfaces; supplement with
  citation searching backward and forward (snowballing, pearl growing), hand
  searching of key journals and proceedings, author contact and grey
  literature; stop when new searches return nothing new; record every search
  with database, interface, date, full string, filters and hit count.
- Reference management from day one: import all hits, deduplicate, keep the
  record of every decision.
- Screening in two stages, first titles and abstracts, then full texts,
  against inclusion and exclusion criteria written before screening; a second
  screener on at least a sample; reasons recorded for every full-text
  exclusion.
- Appraisal: the hierarchy of evidence is a starting point only; use a
  checklist suited to the study design (CASP and others); appraisal serves to
  weight and interpret, not only to exclude.
- Synthesis: extract into tables with common fields first, then choose
  narrative, tabular, thematic, framework, realist or statistical synthesis
  according to the question and the kind of data.
- Analysis goes beyond summary: patterns, contradictions, gaps; state what is
  known, what is uncertain and what is missing.
- Writing up: methods reported so the review can be repeated; a protocol as
  the first written product; a flow diagram of records; a plan for updating.

## What we take from it

- A thesis literature review is systematic in method and narrative in
  synthesis: it borrows the protocol, the logged search, the two-stage
  screen and the extraction table without claiming to be a systematic
  review.
- Search log fields: database, interface, date, full query string, filters,
  hit count, and the deduplicated total across databases.
- Define the review question in a framework before searching; for method
  papers in our field the blocks are task, data, method, comparison, metric.
- Build the extraction table before the synthesis and synthesize by concept,
  not paper by paper.
- Every full-text exclusion has a recorded reason.
