# The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint: Pitching Out Corrupts Within

Source: Tufte ER (2006) The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint: Pitching Out Corrupts Within, 2nd ed. Graphics Press
Status: drafted from general knowledge of the work on 2026-09-02; cite-only book; Robert to review and correct.

## Main ideas

- A polemic essay arguing that the default conventions of slideware impose a
  cognitive style on content: low resolution per slide, a relentless
  sequential ordering that hides relationships, deep bullet hierarchies that
  fragment reasoning, and a preoccupation with format over substance.
- Bullet outlines strip out the connective words (because, therefore,
  unless) that carry an argument, so causal and comparative reasoning is
  lost; the audience sees a list of assertions without their relations.
- Slides carry very little information compared with a printed page; a
  typical slide holds a few dozen words where a page holds hundreds, so a
  talk built from slides forces the speaker to chop an argument into small
  pieces and loses the comparisons that need to be seen side by side.
- Case study: the Columbia shuttle engineering slide, where a critical
  finding about foam-strike damage was buried at the fourth level of a
  bullet hierarchy under a reassuring title. Tufte argues that the format
  contributed to the misreading of the risk.
- Chartjunk and decorative templates (logos on every slide, gradients,
  clip art, animations) consume space and attention without adding data.
- Tables and graphics on slides are routinely too sparse: a few numbers in
  a large font where a proper table would show the full comparison. Tufte
  favors high-resolution visuals that let the audience make their own
  comparisons.
- Recommended alternative: for technical content, give the audience a
  well-designed printed handout (he suggests a dense two-sided page) and
  use projected material only for what benefits from being shown, such as
  images and demonstrations; when slides are used, they should show
  evidence, not talking points.
- The style of presentation shapes the style of thinking: organizations
  that communicate in bullet fragments start to reason in fragments.

## What we take from it

- Bullet hierarchies hide the argument; where a slide needs reasoning, write
  the sentence that states the relation instead of a fragment.
- Never bury a critical result under a reassuring title; the title must
  state what the evidence shows, including bad news.
- Prefer one figure or table with enough resolution to allow comparison
  over several sparse slides; do not chop a comparison across slides.
- Cut decoration and template chrome; every element on a slide is either
  evidence or noise.
- For dense technical detail, a handout or the paper is the right medium; the
  slides carry the message and the evidence for it.
