# Writing for Computer Science

Source: Zobel J (2014) Writing for Computer Science, 3rd ed. Springer
Status: drafted from general knowledge of the work on 2026-09-02; cite-only book; Robert to review and correct.

## Main ideas

- A handbook for computer scientists on reading, doing and reporting
  research, with chapters on reading and reviewing, hypotheses and evidence,
  paper structure, style, mathematics, algorithms, graphs and tables,
  experimentation, statistics, refereeing, ethics and presentations.
- Reading: read skeptically; publication does not make a paper correct;
  separate what was shown from what is claimed; find literature by following
  references backward, citation indexes forward and browsing the main venues
  of the field; keep an annotated bibliography while reading; a literature
  review evaluates and connects, it does not list.
- Citations: cite only what you have read; never cite a source for a claim it
  does not make; cite the original source of an idea rather than a later
  paper that repeats it; prefer archival sources (journals, refereed
  proceedings, theses) to web pages, and give an access date when a web page
  is unavoidable; every reference complete and accurate (authors, title,
  venue, year, pages or identifier) and checked against the original;
  consistent format; name authors in the sentence rather than treating a
  bracketed number as a noun; no padding of reference lists, restrained
  self-citation, no citations in the abstract.
- Structure: title, abstract, introduction, body, conclusions. The abstract
  states problem, approach and result; the introduction gives motivation,
  contribution and how the contribution is demonstrated; the body follows
  the argument, not the chronology of the work; each result section leads
  with the claim and follows with the evidence; conclusions say what was
  learned and what is open, not a second summary.
- Hypotheses and evidence: state the hypothesis so that it can be false;
  evidence by proof, model, simulation or experiment; baselines and fair
  comparison; describe data, parameters and hardware so that others can
  repeat; report negative results; do not select the favorable runs.
- Figures and tables: every one referenced in the text with a self-contained
  caption; axes labeled with units; the plot type follows the message (lines
  for trends, bars for categories, log scales for wide ranges); no clutter,
  no decorative 3D; tables for exact values, aligned, with few rules; fonts
  consistent with the text.
- Algorithms: pseudocode at one level of abstraction rather than real code;
  input, output and preconditions stated; nontrivial steps explained in
  prose; complexity given; source code only when the code itself is the
  contribution.
- Mathematics: define every symbol before use; consistent notation; number
  only equations that are referred to; formulas embedded in sentences; do not
  formalize what plain prose says better; standard proofs sketched, not
  reproduced.
- Ethics: plagiarism including reuse of one's own text; fabrication and
  falsification; authorship only for intellectual contribution; duplicate
  publication and salami slicing; confidentiality of manuscripts under
  review; citations used to flatter or inflate are a form of misconduct.
- Refereeing: read the whole paper; summarize it to show understanding;
  judge contribution, correctness, novelty and presentation; separate major
  from minor issues; be specific and constructive; check that references
  exist and say what is claimed; declare conflicts and do not review work
  one cannot judge fairly.

## What we take from it

- Cite what you have read, from the primary source, for what it actually
  says; check every entry against the original before submission.
- Archival sources over web pages; web pages with access date.
- Lead with the claim, follow with the evidence, in prose, figures and
  algorithms alike; every figure and table earns its caption and its
  reference in the text.
- Pseudocode, not code, unless code is the contribution.
- A referee report checks the references too.
