# At the Helm: Leading Your Laboratory

Source: Barker K (2010) At the Helm: Leading Your Laboratory, 2nd ed. Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory Press
Status: drafted from general knowledge of the work on 2026-09-02; cite-only book; Robert to review and correct.

## Main ideas

- The book is a practical handbook for the scientist who has just become a
  principal investigator. Its premise is that running a lab is a management
  job that nobody trained you for, and that the lab will take on the
  character of its head whether or not the head chooses that character
  deliberately.
- Getting started: decide early what kind of lab you want (size, pace,
  style of supervision) and write down the values and the working rules that
  follow from it. A lab without stated rules still has rules; they are just
  unspoken and inconsistent.
- Hiring is the most consequential decision a lab head makes. Barker treats
  it as a process: define the role before advertising, interview for fit
  and motivation as well as skills, talk to references directly, involve
  the current lab in the visit, and be slow to hire and quick to notice when
  a hire is not working.
- Expectations belong in writing and belong at the start. New members should
  know within their first weeks what the lab expects about working hours,
  notebooks and data, authorship, presenting at lab meetings, and how and
  when they will hear from the PI.
- Communication runs on two channels: regular individual meetings, where the
  PI learns what is actually happening and the member gets undivided
  attention, and group meetings, which serve to share results, get
  criticism, and build a common culture. Barker discusses several lab
  meeting formats (data presentations, journal clubs, project reviews) and
  advises picking a format and keeping the schedule.
- Mentoring differs by role. Graduate students need a path to a degree and
  protection from drifting projects; postdocs need independence and a
  visible route to their own position; technicians need clear roles and
  recognition. Motivating each group requires knowing what each wants.
- Problems must be addressed early and directly. Barker gives concrete
  guidance for difficult conversations: prepare, be specific about behavior
  rather than character, document what was said and agreed, and follow up.
  Avoidance is the most common failure of new lab heads.
- Conflict, harassment, misconduct, and departures each have institutional
  procedures; the PI should know them before they are needed and should
  involve the institution rather than improvise.
- Scientific responsibility is the PI's. This covers lab notebooks and data
  ownership, authorship rules discussed openly and in advance, and a lab
  culture in which errors are reported rather than hidden.
- Time management for the PI: protect writing time, delegate what others can
  do, and accept that managing people is part of the job rather than an
  interruption of it.
- When people leave, plan the hand-over of data, reagents, code, and
  unfinished manuscripts; the exit is part of the management cycle, not
  an afterthought.

## What we take from it

- Write the lab's working rules down once and give them to every newcomer;
  the meeting-scribe and onboarding material should point to that document
  rather than restate it.
- Keep a regular individual meeting with every student and staff member, and
  keep a group meeting on a fixed schedule. Format matters less than
  consistency.
- Every individual meeting leaves a written trace: what was discussed, what
  was agreed, and what is due next. Barker's advice on documenting difficult
  conversations applies to ordinary ones too; the record makes the hard
  conversation easier when it comes.
- Notice problems early. The group-monitor briefing exists to surface stalled
  work while a conversation is still cheap.
- Hiring, misconduct, and departures follow institutional procedure; the
  skills should remind the PI of the procedure, not replace it.
- Treat hand-over at departure as a checklist item, tied to the contract end
  date in the roster.
