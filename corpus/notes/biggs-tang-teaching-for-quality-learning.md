# Teaching for Quality Learning at University

Source: Biggs J, Tang C (2011) Teaching for Quality Learning at University, 4th ed. Open University Press / McGraw-Hill
Status: drafted from general knowledge of the work on 2026-09-02; cite-only book; Robert to review and correct.

## Main ideas

- Constructive alignment joins two ideas: students construct meaning
  through what they do (constructivism), and the teacher aligns the intended
  learning outcomes, the teaching and learning activities and the assessment
  tasks so that they all point at the same verbs. The outcome states what
  the student will be able to do; the activities make the student do it; the
  assessment checks whether it was done and how well.
- Intended learning outcomes are written with a verb at a chosen level of
  understanding, the content the verb acts on, and the context or standard.
  The verb is the hinge: "describe" and "apply" and "design" ask for
  different things, and the assessment must ask for the same verb.
- The SOLO taxonomy (structure of the observed learning outcome) grades the
  quality of a response in five levels: prestructural, unistructural (one
  relevant aspect), multistructural (several aspects, unrelated),
  relational (aspects integrated into a structure) and extended abstract
  (generalized to new domains). Verbs can be mapped to SOLO levels, which
  lets outcomes and assessment tasks be compared for level.
- Surface and deep approaches to learning are responses to the teaching
  environment rather than fixed student traits. Assessment that rewards
  recall, heavy workload and unclear goals push students to surface
  learning; aligned tasks that require understanding pull them to deep
  learning.
- Assessment drives student behavior: students learn what they think will
  be assessed (backwash). The teacher should therefore design the
  assessment so that the backwash is the intended learning.
- Criterion-referenced assessment judges each student against the outcome
  standard, not against classmates. Grading should describe how well the
  outcome was met (holistic, using rubrics with level descriptors), rather
  than adding marks for unrelated items. Norm-referenced grading and
  excessive quantification are criticized.
- Teaching and learning activities are chosen for what they make students
  do: large-class techniques (peer instruction, buzz groups, minute papers),
  small-group work, problem-based learning, and independent study each
  activate specific verbs.
- Implementing constructive alignment across a program means aligning
  course outcomes with program outcomes and graduate attributes, and
  reviewing courses through the same lens.

## What we take from it

- Every intended learning outcome in course.yaml carries a verb; the
  alignment matrix compares the outcome verb's level with the verbs of the
  assessments and activities that claim it.
- An outcome with no assessment and an assessment that maps to no outcome
  are both design errors; the alignment script fails on them.
- Assessment tasks are criterion-referenced with published rubrics; grades
  describe attainment of outcomes, not rank in the class.
- Because assessment drives behavior, design the assessment before the
  lectures and check that its backwash is the learning we want.
- SOLO levels are a useful common scale for comparing outcome verbs across
  outcomes, assessments and activities; the group uses them alongside the
  revised Bloom verbs.
