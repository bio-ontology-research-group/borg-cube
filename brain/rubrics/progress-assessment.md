# Rubric: progress assessment (Robert-only)

Used by the advisor for weekly digests and pre-1:1 briefs. Every score cites
an evidence id (commit, file, org heading with date, pa status date). A
dimension without evidence is "no evidence", not a low score. Output is
`privacy:local-only`.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research question and plan | none stated | stated, no success criteria | question, hypotheses, kill criteria written | proposal, org notes |
| Evidence velocity | no artefact in 21 days | artefacts, irregular | weekly commits or draft changes | commits, drafts |
| Methodological rigour | no baselines, no seeds | baselines, partial tracking | baselines, seeds, data versions, run manifest | repo, notebooks |
| Writing progress | nothing written | notes only | draft section or chapter with figures | paper directory |
| Milestone standing | rule deadline missed or unknown | on estimate, no artefact | artefact exists for the next milestone | kaust_rules.py, staff.org |
| Communication | no meeting entry in 21 days, no replies | irregular | regular meetings with recorded action items | org file |
| Independence | needs direction each step | proposes next steps | proposes and executes plan, asks well-formed questions | org notes, check-ins |

Guidance: a progress review supports a conversation, it does not replace one
(NAP 2019, Science of Effective Mentorship in STEMM). The Vitae Researcher
Development Framework domain A (knowledge and intellectual abilities) frames
the first three rows; KAUST CEMSE criteria frame milestone standing. Good
research and software practice rows draw on Schwab et al. 2022 (ten simple
rules for good research practice), Wilson et al. 2017 (good enough practices
in scientific computing) and Schnell 2015 (lab notebook rules). Question
quality follows the revised Bloom taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl 2001).
Sutherland et al. 2013 (twenty tips for interpreting scientific claims)
informs the rigour row. Marino et al. 2014 (finishing your PhD) and Gu and
Bourne 2007 (graduate students) inform the independence row.

Never: rank students against each other; show any row to a student; infer a
reason for low velocity (illness, motivation) without the student having said
it.
